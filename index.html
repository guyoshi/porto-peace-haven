Room Guides

🌉

Ribeira

Elegant, spacious and urban — the soul of old Porto

📷 Room photos

The deep blue of the Douro at dusk. The largest of the three rooms — room to breathe, and room to work without rushing.

🛏️ For your comfort
  • A bed dressed with two pillows — one higher, one lower — so you sleep the way that suits you.
  • A fan, and windows that open wide to air and cool the room (there is no air conditioning, but Porto comes in nicely).
  • A mini fridge for whatever you would like to keep close.
💻 For working
  • A proper office chair — not a dining chair pretending to be one.
  • A wide shelf built into the wall that doubles as a desk — made for remote work and longer stays.
💡 Light, sound & screen
  • Two RGB LED lamps plus an LED strip behind the TV, all remote-controlled — set the colour and the mood you feel like tonight.
  • A TV with an Android player: Netflix, HBO Max, Prime Video, Disney+ with your own accounts — just remember to log out before check-out.
🧳 Storage & details
  • Generous storage plus a clothes rail, with plenty of room for your suitcase.
  • Two framed prints of the Ribeira, chosen with this room in mind.
  • Sockets with USB-A and USB-C, and a full-length mirror.

We prepared this room down to the smallest detail, so you would rest better. If anything is not the way you hoped, tell us now — we would rather put it right during your stay than after it.

Kitchen Guide

How to use everything in the kitchen

🍳

Induction Hob

  1. Place a compatible (magnetic) pan on the hob surface.
  2. Press the power button to switch on.
  3. Select your zone and adjust heat with the + / – controls.
  4. The hob switches itself off automatically after 2 hours.
💨

Extractor Hood

  1. Switch on before cooking to prevent smoke build-up.
  2. Adjust speed with the button (1 = low · 3 = high).
  3. Leave running briefly after cooking, then switch off.
🥐

Mini Oven

  1. Place food on the tray or rack provided.
  2. Set temperature using the left dial.
  3. Set cooking time using the right dial.
  4. Oven switches off automatically when timer ends.
📡

Microwave

  1. Use only microwave-safe containers.
  2. Set time and power, then press start.
  3. Never use metal containers or foil.
🫖

Kettle

  1. Fill with water up to the maximum line indicated inside.
  2. Place on the base and press the switch down.
  3. It switches off automatically once boiled.
☕

Capsule Coffee Machine

  1. Fill the water tank and switch the machine on.
  2. Insert a compatible capsule into the holder.
  3. Place your cup and press brew.
  4. Remove and dispose of used capsule after each use.
🍴

What's Available

Plates, bowls, glasses, mugs, cups, cutlery, pots, pans, cutting boards, knives, colander, grater, and cooking utensils.

🧹

Cleaning Supplies

Bin bags, sponge, cloths, and washing-up liquid are under the sink. Extra supplies are in the storage cabinet near the bathroom.

Kitchen Etiquette

  • 🍽️ Wash and dry all dishes immediately after every use.
  • 🧹 Wipe down the hob, surfaces and sink after use.
  • 🗑️ Dispose of rubbish in the bin bags provided. Sealed bags go to the building's waste collection — message us if you would like the exact spot.
  • ♻️ Please separate recycling — yellow for plastic and metal, blue for paper, green for glass — using the public recycling bins on the street.
  • 🤝 This is a shared kitchen — leave it exactly as you found it.

Get to Know Porto

Beyond the postcards — the places that make us love living here

Porto isn't a city you tick off a list. It rewards the wanderers — the ones who turn down the narrow street, who sit on the steps a while, who order the thing they can't pronounce. Here are the places we'd send a friend to: a few that everyone knows, and a few that most visitors walk straight past.

Porto off the beaten path

🌀 Different $$$$$

Wondersense — the museum of the five senses

A few steps from Livraria Lello, five floors of rooms built to be tasted, heard, touched and smelled. Sixteen experiences, no two alike — the kind of hour that makes adults forget to check their phones. Book a time slot ahead.

Go →
🍷 Curious $$$$$

Capela Incomum — wine in a chapel

A small wine bar inside a former chapel — the altar is still there. Low light, a quiet glass of Douro red, and the strange, lovely feeling of a toast under a vaulted ceiling. Not somewhere you stumble into by accident.

Go →
🌳 Sunset FREE

Passeio das Virtudes — the local sunset

While everyone crowds Jardim do Morro across the river, locals come here: a garden that tumbles down the hillside in terraces, with the largest ginkgo tree in Portugal and an open view over the Douro. Bring something to drink and stay for the colour of the sky.

Go →
🌉 View FREE

Escadas dos Guindais — under the bridge

A steep, narrow staircase that drops down the cliff right beneath the Dom Luís I Bridge. You've seen the bridge in every postcard — here you stand under its iron belly, close enough to hear it. Take it slow on the way down.

Go →
🔵 Azulejos FREE

Capela das Almas — a chapel of blue tiles

On busy Rua de Santa Catarina, stop and look up: sixteen thousand blue azulejos wrap the whole outside of this chapel like a story told on the wall. Most people walk past it on the way to the shops. Don't.

Go →
📷 Free FREE

Centro Português de Fotografia — the old prison

A photography museum housed in a 19th-century prison — you can still see the cell doors and the iron walkways. Free to enter, rarely crowded, and quietly one of the most atmospheric buildings in the city.

Go →

The classics, taken slowly

📚 Classic $$$$$

Livraria Lello — go early

Yes, it's famous, and yes, there's a queue. Worth it — but buy the timed ticket online and go right at opening or in the last hour, when the carved staircase is almost yours. The ticket price comes off a book.

Go →
🌅 Must-see FREE

Cross the bridge on the top deck

Walk the upper deck of the Dom Luís I Bridge — the metro shares it, but there's room, and the view stops you mid-step. On the Gaia side, Jardim do Morro is the postcard sunset. Go — just go early to find a spot.

Go →
🏛️ The heart FREE

Get lost in the Ribeira

The oldest part of Porto, right on the water — tilting houses, laundry strung between windows, alleys that lead nowhere and everywhere. Touristy, yes. Still the soul of the city. Go in the morning, before the boats fill up.

Go →
🍷 Taste $$$$$

Cross to Gaia for a port tasting

The port wine cellars line the Gaia bank, ten minutes' walk over the bridge. Pick one of the smaller houses for an unhurried tasting — they'll explain the difference between a tawny and a ruby, and you'll never forget it.

Go →

Where to eat

🥪 Cheap eats $$$$$

Conga — the legendary bifana

A tiny, no-frills institution since 1976. The bifana — slow-cooked pork in a soft roll — is one of the cheapest great meals in Porto.

Go →
🍽️ Near & traditional $$$$$

Capa Negra II — dinner near your door

A reliable, busy local restaurant a short walk from the apartment. Grilled meats, fish, and a serious francesinha.

Go →
🍷 Mid-range gem $$$$$

Tapabento — small place, big reputation

Modern Portuguese cooking and petiscos done with real care. Small, warm, and consistently excellent.

Go →
✨ Special occasion $$$$$

Antiqvvm — a Michelin-starred evening

Fine-dining Portuguese cuisine in a romantic 19th-century house with a Douro view. A genuine occasion.

Go →
